# PDR Auditing

PROVIDES an audit methodology verifying PDRs declare well-formed, observable product decisions
SO THAT all product-tree projects
CAN eliminate malformed product decisions before they accumulate

## Assertions

### Audit: classification

- six-content-types: ALWAYS: classify every PDR statement into at least six content types — product behavior, observable non-functional property, technology choice, implementation approach, data structure, performance implementation.
- audience-grounded-observable: ALWAYS: ground "observable" in the product document's declared audience and interaction surfaces rather than a fixed end-user-application assumption — a statement is product behavior when that audience observes or operates it, so an audience that operates the product through a command-line, filesystem, or version-control surface makes those mechanics product behavior, while the internal algorithm, in-memory data structure, persisted schema, and library choices that audience never operates remain architecture.
- classification-first: ALWAYS: check content classification as the first audit phase — a PDR full of architecture content fails regardless of other properties.
- can-rely-on-observable: ALWAYS: verify the behavior the CAN RELY ON line names is observable and falsifiable from the declared audience's perspective, not the implementation's, and holds at failure and boundary conditions.
- consistency-compared: ALWAYS: compare the PDR against the product document and ancestor PDRs; a contradiction with either is a consistency violation.

### Audit: declaration form

- ungrouped-rule: ALWAYS: a rule under `## Verification` that stands under no typed group heading is a `declaration-form` finding.
- rule-type-fit: ALWAYS: verify each rule's assertion type, named by its group heading, fits the claim's quantifier per the `/test` router — a universal is never `scenario`; reject a type the router would not produce, without relitigating a choice the router leaves open.
- approval-is-not-evidence: NEVER: approval of a PDR declaration establishes that its rules have evidence or that the product exhibits the declared behavior.

### Audit: context and recording

- record-as-judged: ALWAYS: `/audit-pdr` records the root unit as soon as the run starts, each other unit as soon as its judgment completes, and each finding on touched text as soon as it is raised and its unit is recorded, so no unit or finding waits for the judgment of a later unit.
- read-only-context: ALWAYS: before any audit phase, load read-only at the audited snapshot the complete Walk to the Node that holds the PDR — every ancestor spec from the root, at each level along the path every sibling spec and each Decision Record indexed below the child the path continues through, every `ISSUES.md` along the path, the published contracts of the Node's immediate children, the Node's own Decision Records and spec, its Knowledge `index.md` and its Outcome record — then each decision a loaded spec or decision cites from another subtree, once; judge the PDR as the subject, read every other loaded decision for awareness, and take as governing the PDR only a decision that governs it by Index, scope or citation: one at a lower Index than the PDR in its own directory, one at a lower Index than the child the path continues through at an ancestor level, or one that a loaded spec or a governing record cites for it from another subtree; run no command that fetches, rebases, or moves the checkout, so the audit changes no checkout state.
- touched-text-only: ALWAYS: the PDR auditor rejects only on touched text under [`spx/31-outcomeeng.enabler/31-verification.enabler/14-verification.pdr.md`](spx/31-outcomeeng.enabler/31-verification.enabler/14-verification.pdr.md), returns each other finding as `filed`, and approves when no touched finding exists.
- run-recording: ALWAYS: `/audit-pdr` records its audit through `spx verification run` on a file scope anchored on the PDR path: one root unit with `auditKind` `pdr`, one child per evidence-model property, and every finding. It returns the run token, the run file, and the rendered projection, or a blocked diagnostic when spx refuses a recorded payload or the finish, and the `pdr-auditor` wrapper passes the raw target and its run-driver identity and relays them unchanged.
